### 2026-06-30 — follow-up dump (relics, the First 9, class commitment)

**(1) The relics — 6 console pieces** *(concept art: `assets/console-pieces-concept.jpg`)*. Each piece is
scattered, in the possession of various characters, **disguised/repurposed** as some other object whose
true nature is unknown — e.g. the **Controller = a necklace** worn by the **Frogadier leader**, believed
to be merely "an object of great power" (this is *why the bad guys attack at the opening*). Likely **5
pieces reassemble the console + 1 cartridge activates its power** — count adjustable as the story develops.
The six (from the concept art):
> 1. **Controller** — frog necklace · 2. **Shell** — shoulder armor · 3. **Motherboard** — chest ·
> 4. **AV cable** — nunchuck · 5. **Power cable** — nunchuck · 6. **Game Cartridge** — holy grail (the activator).

**(2) The First 9 — full myth.** The nine are **one each of: Hare, Otter, Badger, Mouse, Frog, Turtle, Fox,
Weasel, Wildcat.** They were the first to evolve to higher intelligence, **drawn together by a mysterious
power** to one place. Discovering they weren't alone, they built the **first community** — architecture,
written & spoken language, agriculture — everything the real world did, but **accelerated**. As their
teachings spread and more "altered critters" awakened, the Nine found their **aging had slowed** (very long
lives). Then **3 of the 9 split from the other 6**: the **6** wanted to improve the world for all creatures;
the **3 (Fox, Weasel, Wildcat)** wanted to hoard their secrets and make their **immortality permanent**. The
3 left with whatever kin would follow, seeking power. The 6 built peace for years — unprepared for the 3's
**return**: each of the 3 had found **missing relic pieces** granting power and command over followers; they
raised **3 armies**, united, and **assaulted the 6's community**. The settlement was **destroyed**, followers
scattered, and **5 of the 6 elders killed** (the **Frog elder survived** → the starting mission). The 3 took
control of the world — harvesting resources, enslaving free folk, razing the civilization — for
**generations**, excavating sites with forced labor to find **more console pieces**. It is all led by the
**Wildcat**, holder of the **golden Game Cartridge** (the source of the power). By PC's arrival the
**resistance is on its last legs**, desperate for a hero to rise against the Wildcat — **PC may be the
foretold hero.**

**(3) Class commitment is permanent — NO respec.** The post-trial choice is final. *(Considered, then
redirected:)* rather than a relic that lets you swap **class** at will, a relic piece should instead enable
**free TALENT-point reallocation (out of combat only)** — leaving class commitment meaningful.

### 2026-06-30 — follow-up dump (knowledge asymmetry, races)

**(1) Who knows what about the console.** There's a deliberate **knowledge gap**: only **leaders** on both
sides understand the relics' true nature. **Non-leaders on both sides** believe the opening event was a
**"summoning" spell**. In reality it was an **incomplete spell channeled from afar** whose **unintended
side-effect** was opening the portal and pulling PC into this world. (So the "great Evil spirit" is in-world
misinformation, not a real separate entity — dramatic irony the player is in on.)

**(2) Races — fate vs. reality, and the playable roster.** The First 9 weren't chosen for virtue; they were
"**selected by fate**" — *which in reality means they were the **playable races on the magical game
cartridge.*** There are **good AND bad creatures of every race** (race ≠ alignment). The world also holds
many more peoples beyond the Nine: **Squirrels, Moles, Hedgehogs, Voles, merfolk, fae (fairies), canines,
ferrets, bird-folk, and more.** **At launch, playable races = the First 9.** **Additional races** (ferret,
rat, dog-folk, bird-folk, Mole, bear, more) are **unlockable for future playthroughs by completing parts of
the campaign.** Exact bestiary — which creatures fit this world and which don't — to be fleshed out later.

### 1. Premise & logline
✅ PC begins as an **ordinary household/farm critter** in the "real" world, moving into a new home with
the family they belong to. Their best friend is the family's **toddler child ("the Kid")**. A hidden
**Coraline-style portal** to another world is awakening in the new house — sensed only by PC and the Kid.
When an **external force** tries to pull the Kid through, PC **takes the child's place**, is dragged in,
and **transforms during transit** (→ this *is* the character-creation screen). PC awakens as the
**created character** in a beautiful fairytale wilderness — landing mid-disaster through a dark summoning
sky into a **Frogadier** camp. The emotional spine: PC will do **whatever it takes to get back to the
Kid**, and along the way commits to **saving this new world** and its people.

✅ **The portal is an old video game that isekais PC.** *(Decided 2026-06-30.)* This is now load-bearing
lore, not flavor — it ties directly to the relics and the First 9 (see §6): the way **in** and the way
**home** are both *the video game console itself.* A meta resonance with the slot-reel combat hook.

### 2. Themes & tone
✅ **Themes:** Belonging · finding your place in the world · found/chosen family · redemption · love.
✅ **Tone:** *mildly grim with real stakes* but deliberately **not** dwelling in bloodshed/darkness — the
through-line is **finding the good in all things and the beauty of the world.** Redemption of enemies is a
recurring beat, not just defeating them.
✅ **Visual touchstone:** **Paper Mario** ("extremely like"). 🔬 Pairs naturally with the world-research
recommendation of a hub + linear-chapter spine (Paper Mario / classic FF) in [[11-world-and-overworld]].
📖 Redwall lineage stays in the *anthropomorphic-folk-vs-corruption* DNA, but **villains are not a race** —
a key departure from Redwall's vermin-as-species (see §6).

### 3. Story structure
✅ **Mostly linear** with **many smaller branching beats** driven by PC choices — explicitly **KOTOR +
Mass Effect** as the model (linear spine, meaningful local choices, companion arcs).
✅ **Length target: 8–12 chapters**, flexing with how the writing condenses for playtime.
🔬 Fits the **hub + linear chapters** spine (each chapter culminating in a designed boss) — see
[[11-world-and-overworld]] · [[28-encounter-design-framework]].
✅ **First boss = tutorial boss** with an **obvious telegraphed weakness** (expected of a tutorial); later
bosses hide their weaknesses (see §8).

### 4. Player-character framing
✅ **Created, named PC (blank-ish vessel).** The transformation-in-transit canonically *justifies* the
creation screen, and the **KOTOR companion model** (chosen earlier) lets companions carry the authored
personality. Settles the §9 hinge from the template.
✅ **Outlander identity** — PC (and secretly [ORG Leader]) are **Outlanders**: people pulled from other
worlds, with **innate versatility** — faster growth, faster skill acquisition.
✅ **Class commitment via a "trial" window, then PERMANENT** *(revised 2026-06-30 — supersedes
free-swap-anytime).* After the class tutorial, the **first quest line** lets PC **swap classes freely**
(a try-before-you-buy period). On completing that quest line, PC makes a **final, committed class choice
that is NOT re-spec-able** — the commitment gives the build real weight. The Outlander trait canonically
explains the early fluidity.
✅ **The "free-swap" relic is redirected to talents, not class.** Rather than a relic that swaps class at
will, a relic piece **enables free TALENT-point reallocation (out of combat only)** — preserving the
permanence of the class choice while keeping builds tinkerable. → §8, [[23-talents-and-reel-points]].
Big cross-system lock → [[12-companions-and-party]] · [[20-character-creation]] · [[22-leveling]] · classes.

### 6. Factions & the world's powers
✅ **Core conflict:** all **innocent/civilized folk (the good)** vs. **outcast/evil tribes & groups** —
those **corrupted by dark magic** or **greedy/violent enough to be cast out**. Crucially **not one
species** — a corrupted-soul axis, enabling the redemption theme.
✅ **[ORG]** — a **militia** that protects the scattered hidden settlements; led by **[ORG Leader]**, who
is themselves a **secret Outlander**. PC's entry faction.
✅ **Lore — the First 9 (the founding myth).** Nine creatures — **one each of Hare, Otter, Badger, Mouse,
Frog, Turtle, Fox, Weasel, Wildcat** — were the first to awaken to higher intelligence, **drawn together by
a mysterious power**. They founded the **first community**, building architecture, written & spoken
language, and agriculture at an **accelerated pace**, and as more "altered critters" awakened, their
teachings spread. The Nine then discovered their **aging had slowed** — near-immortal lifespans.
🔑 **The twist (player knowledge):** the Nine weren't "selected by fate" for virtue — **they are the
playable races encoded on the magical Game Cartridge.** The myth in-world dresses this up as destiny.
✅ **The Schism (6 vs 3).** The Nine split: the **6 (Hare, Otter, Badger, Mouse, Frog, Turtle)** wanted to
**improve the world for all creatures**; the **3 (Fox, Weasel, Wildcat)** wanted to **hoard their secrets
and make their immortality permanent**. The 3 left with their followers to seek power.
⚠️ **Race ≠ alignment.** The three founding villains *happened* to be Fox/Weasel/Wildcat **individuals** —
this does **NOT** make those races evil. There are **good and bad creatures of every race**; the conflict is
the corruption/greed axis (§ premise), never species.
✅ **The Return & the Fall.** The 3 each found **missing relic pieces** that granted power and command over
followers; they raised **3 armies**, united, and **destroyed the 6's settlement** — scattering its people
and **killing 5 of the 6 elders**. The **Frog elder survived** (→ the opening Frogadier mission). The 3 have
ruled for **generations** since: harvesting resources, **enslaving free folk**, and using **forced labor to
excavate for more console pieces**.
✅ **The present antagonist — the Wildcat.** The takeover is led by the **Wildcat of the First 9**, holder of
the **golden Game Cartridge** — the **source of the power**. The **resistance is on its last legs**; PC may
be the **foretold hero** who can finally challenge the Wildcat.
✅ **Knowledge asymmetry (resolves the "great Evil spirit").** Only **leaders** on either side know the
relics' true nature. **Non-leaders on both sides** believe the opening event was a **"summoning" spell** for
a great Evil — but there is **no separate spirit.** What actually happened: an **incomplete spell channeled
from afar** whose **unintended side-effect** tore the portal and pulled PC into this world. The "summoning"
is in-world misinformation; the player is in on the irony. The real antagonist is simply the **console's
power wielded by the Wildcat.**
✅ **MacGuffin — the relics are 6 pieces of a video game console** *(concept: `assets/console-pieces-concept.jpg`).*
Each is **disguised/repurposed** and scattered among various holders, its true nature unknown:
> **1. Controller** → Frogadier leader's **necklace** (the opening attack's true prize) · **2. Shell** →
> shoulder armor · **3. Motherboard** → chest piece · **4. AV cable** → nunchuck · **5. Power cable** →
> nunchuck · **6. Game Cartridge** → the "holy grail" activator (held by the Wildcat).
**5 pieces reassemble the console; the 6th (cartridge) activates its power.** Assembled, it forms the **link
between this world and the real world** — i.e. **PC's path home.** So the world-saving quest (take the
cartridge from the Wildcat) and PC's personal stake (get home to the Kid) are the **same hunt**.
🟦 *Piece count adjustable as the story develops; chapter-mapping + any held-piece mechanics still open (§9).*

✅ **Races & the playable roster.** The world is full of peoples beyond the Nine — **Squirrels, Moles,
